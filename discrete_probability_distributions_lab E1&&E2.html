Laboratory Modules

Active Module

Binomial Distribution

Models the number of successes $X$ in $n$ independent Bernoulli trials with success probability $p$.

$$P(X=k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k}$$
Active Module

Poisson Distribution

Models event counts occurring within a fixed interval given a constant mean rate parameter $\lambda$.

$$P(X = k) = \frac{\lambda^k e^{-\lambda}}{k!}$$
Active Module

Geometric Distribution

Models the number of trials $X$ required to achieve the first success with probability $p$.

$$P(X = k) = (1-p)^{k-1} p$$

Detailed solutions and interactive verification for Exercise 1 & Exercise 2

Exercise 1 – Interactive Binomial Distribution UI

Configuration: Number of trials $n = 15$, Success rate $p = 0.6$

1. Mean and Variance

Mean $\mu = np = 15 \times 0.6 = \mathbf{9.0000}$
Variance $\sigma^2 = np(1-p) = 15 \times 0.6 \times 0.4 = \mathbf{3.6000}$

2. $P(X = 9)$ — Exactly 9 Successes

$P(X = 9) = \binom{15}{9} (0.6)^9 (0.4)^6 = \mathbf{0.2066}$ ($20.66\%$)

3. $P(X \le 10)$ — At Most 10 Successes

$P(X \le 10) = \sum_{j=0}^{10} P(X = j) = \mathbf{0.7827}$ ($78.27\%$)

4. PMF Graph Peak Region

The highest probability peak occurs precisely at **$X = 9$** with $P(X=9) \approx 0.2066$, coinciding exactly with the theoretical mean $\mu = 9.0$.

5. Visualization Inference

Because $p = 0.6 > 0.5$, the PMF exhibits a slight negative (left) skewness ($\gamma_1 \approx -0.1054$). The probabilities tail off more gradually toward zero than toward $15$. Since $\mu = 9$ is an integer, $X=9$ is the primary mode.

Exercise 2 – Interactive Poisson Distribution UI

Monitoring System with Mean Event Rate $\lambda = 6$

1. Mean and Variance ($\lambda = 6$)

Mean $\mu = \lambda = \mathbf{6.0000}$
Variance $\sigma^2 = \lambda = \mathbf{6.0000}$

2. $P(X = 8)$ — Exactly 8 Events

$P(X = 8) = \frac{e^{-6} 6^8}{8!} = \mathbf{0.1033}$ ($10.33\%$)

3. $P(X \le 5)$ — At Most 5 Events

$P(X \le 5) = \sum_{j=0}^{5} P(X = j) = \mathbf{0.4457}$ ($44.57\%$)

4. Comparison across $\lambda = 2, 6, 10$

$\lambda = 2$: Mode at $X=1,2$ ($P \approx 0.2707$)Right Skewed
$\lambda = 6$: Mode at $X=5,6$ ($P \approx 0.1606$)Moderate
$\lambda = 10$: Mode at $X=9,10$ ($P \approx 0.1251$)Near Gaussian

5. Visualization Inference

As rate $\lambda$ increases, the Poisson distribution shifts rightward and transitions from heavy right-skewness ($\gamma_1 = 1/\sqrt{\lambda}$) toward a symmetric Gaussian bell shape $\mathcal{N}(\mu=\lambda, \sigma^2=\lambda)$. The peak height flattens as variance spreads out.
